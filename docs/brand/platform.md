# Lakes of Bendakaluru - platform and brief

Status: signed 2026-09-11, with the look set to light, colorful and bold.

## Positioning

A playful way to wander every lake in Bangalore, for anyone curious about the city, that turns a dry government list into something you want to explore.

## Audience

Someone who got a link from a friend, opens it on a phone, and knows three lakes by name: Ulsoor, Bellandur, the one near their home.
They already believe the lakes are mostly dead or dirty.
They want to be surprised: the biggest one they never heard of, the one two streets away, the one that is half apartments now.
They do not want a lecture or a government portal.

## Personality

Curious, not academic.
Playful, not cute.
Honest, not preachy.
Bold, not loud: every screen should look like a print you would hang on a wall.

## Not this

A research portal (the IISc lake information system): tabs, logos, tables, built for officials.
A doom article (civic explainers on lost lakes): guilt first, exploration never.
A generic map app (Google Maps): every lake a grey blob among roads and shops.

## Existing assets

Keep the idea "the city drawn only in its water". It is the one thing no peer has.
Evolve the hero map so it works by touch, not only by mouse.
Keep the lake green and the red-soil color, and give the red a real job: pollution and encroachment.
Kill the record gaps as the main story. They become one filter among many.
Kill the embedded street map on lake pages. It clashes and says nothing.
Kill the dark ground. The site is light, saturated and bold, and reads as an artwork first.
Open the typefaces to the territories.

## Brief

The job: let anyone wander Bangalore's lakes and find the surprising ones - the biggest, the dirtiest, the most eaten into, the nearest.

The person: arrives from a shared link, usually on a phone, with a minute to spare and no goal beyond curiosity.

Remember: "Bangalore has far more lakes than I knew, and each one has a story."

Action: open the map, tap a few lakes, try a filter.

Structure: the map is the site.
The home page opens straight onto the lakes, with collections to jump into (biggest, most polluted, most encroached, near me).
Every lake has its own page worth sharing: a photo or satellite view, its size, its water quality, how much of it is built over.

Look: no dark theme. Super colorful and bold, like an artwork.

Mode: marketing for the opening and lake pages (high expression, one orchestrated moment).
Product rules for the map controls locally (compact filters and search, fast feedback, every state designed).

Constraints:
Next.js 16, MapLibre, static data built by a script.
Water quality and encroachment data are not in the dataset yet; they cover only the lakes that were tested or surveyed, and the design has to show that honestly.
Photos: Wikimedia Commons and satellite views are free. Google Maps photos need a paid key and cannot be stored.
Later, visitors will be able to add and correct lake details, so lake pages should read as a set of fields that can grow.

Success: you open it on a phone, tap around without instructions, and reach "most polluted lakes" in two taps.
